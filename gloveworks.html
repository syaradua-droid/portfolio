GloveWorks: architecting a high-conversion digital engine for a boutique boxing workshop

Turning an underground, word-of-mouth boxing facility into an automated commercial platform — pairing industrial grit with modern fight science.

Role
Lead Product / UX & UI Designer
Tools
Figma
Platform
Responsive Web (1440 / 390)
Core Target
Dual-Persona Conversion

The business challenge

GloveWorks ran entirely on word of mouth and Instagram DMs — no site, no visible pricing, no way for a stranger to actually book a session.

"Every lead came through a DM inbox with no pricing, no schedule, and no way to book — most people gave up before ever getting a reply."

DM bottleneck

Every inquiry required a real-time back-and-forth with a coach who was usually mid-session.

Hidden pricing

No visible cost anywhere, which reads as exclusive to some and simply inaccessible to most.

Lead leakage

Interested people bounced before ever reaching a real conversation.

Two very different people walk in the door

Same facility, same coaches — but the reason someone shows up splits cleanly into two groups with different fears and different goals.

PERSONA A // FIGHT WORKSHOP

Youth & Amateur Fighters

Chasing ring IQ and a competitive record. Wants to be taken seriously, not treated like a beginner tourist.

PERSONA B // ATHLETIC LONGEVITY

35+ Longevity Athletes

Wants rotational power and real conditioning — with zero interest in getting hit. Intimidated by anything that looks like a fight gym.

"Concrete heavy-bag jungle meets 2026 athletic lab — inviting yet intimidating." — the founder's own framing of the core paradox this design had to resolve

What stakeholder discovery shaped

No formal usability testing has happened yet — this reflects direct discovery conversations with the client, whose answers changed real design decisions below.

Stakeholder concernDesign response
Persona B (35+ athletes) would assume any session risked real contact. Added a "100% Non-Contact Assessment" trust badge with a loaner-gear policy stated up front.
Members mostly check the schedule one-handed, on mobile, between other tasks. Added edge-gradient scroll cues and paired capacity badges directly next to each timestamp.
People hesitate to hand over a phone number without knowing why. Updated the label to "Mobile (for automated SMS session confirmation only)."

Outcomes & takeaways

What changed

Booking moved from a real-time DM conversation to an automated, always-on funnel — and the dual-track split meant neither persona had to see content that wasn't for them.

What I learned

Defusing intimidation wasn't a copy problem — it was a structural one. The fix was giving Persona B their own entirely separate path through the page, not softer language on a shared one.

Next steps

Run an actual moderated usability study with real members from both tracks once the site is live — everything above reflects stakeholder discovery with the client, not end-user testing yet.
