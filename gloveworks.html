Architecting a high-conversion engine for a boutique boxing workshop.

Turning an underground, word-of-mouth boxing facility into an automated commercial platform — pairing industrial grit with modern fight science.

RoleLead Product / UX & UI Designer
ToolsFigma
PlatformResponsive web (1440 / 390)
Core targetDual-persona conversion
The business challenge

No site, no pricing, no way to book.

GloveWorks ran entirely on word of mouth and Instagram DMs.

“Every lead came through a DM inbox with no pricing, no schedule, and no way to book — most people gave up before ever getting a reply.”

DM bottleneck

Every inquiry needed a real-time back-and-forth with a coach who was usually mid-session.

Hidden pricing

No visible cost anywhere — exclusive to some, simply inaccessible to most.

Lead leakage

Interested people bounced before ever reaching a real conversation.

Two personas

Two very different people walk in the door.

Same facility, same coaches — but the reason someone shows up splits cleanly into two groups with different fears and goals.

Persona A · Fight workshop

Youth & Amateur Fighters

Chasing ring IQ and a competitive record. Wants to be taken seriously, not treated like a beginner tourist.

Persona B · Athletic longevity

35+ Longevity Athletes

Wants rotational power and real conditioning — with zero interest in getting hit. Intimidated by anything that looks like a fight gym.

“Concrete heavy-bag jungle meets 2026 athletic lab — inviting yet intimidating.”

The founder's own framing of the paradox this design had to resolve

On mobile

Same funnel, restructured for a thumb.

Stacked tracks, single-column schedule

Members mostly check the schedule one-handed, between other tasks. The mobile layout keeps the same real data but stacks the two tracks, collapses the matrix into a single column, and pairs each timestamp with its capacity badge so nobody has to scroll sideways to know if a class is full.

Stakeholder discovery

What the client's answers changed.

No formal usability testing yet — these came from direct discovery conversations with the client.

Outcomes

From DM inbox to always-on funnel.

Outcome

What changed

Booking moved from a real-time DM conversation to an automated, always-on funnel — and the dual-track split meant neither persona saw content that wasn't for them.

Reflection

What I learned

Defusing intimidation wasn't a copy problem — it was structural. The fix was giving Persona B their own path through the page, not softer language on a shared one.

Next steps

What's next

Run a moderated usability study with real members from both tracks once the site is live — everything above is stakeholder discovery, not end-user testing.
