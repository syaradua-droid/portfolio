GameMind

Youth athletics equity & mental wellness — a mobile app for athletes and a coordinator portal for the adults trying to help them, built around one hard constraint: nothing could feel like it was drawing attention to a kid who's struggling.

Role
Lead UX Researcher & Designer
Timeline
4 weeks
Tools
Figma, FigJam, Miro
Platform
Mobile app + responsive web

The problem

"Low-income youth athletes face steep equipment costs and a culture of silence around competitive stress and burnout — and the adults trying to help them have no central system to do it."

Two people, two very different jobs to be done

Marcus Chen, 16

The Determined Competitor

"I shouldn't have to choose between new shoes and helping with rent — but nobody talks about how much pressure we're actually under."

Coach Kendra Vance, 28

The Overextended Advocate

"I can teach a kid how to shoot a free throw. I can't magically produce a pair of shoes or a sports psychologist out of thin air."

Marcus's emotional arc

From silent hesitation to confident pickup — every touchpoint in the app had to actively lower the emotional barrier to asking for help.

1. Recognize the need

Hesitant & isolated

2. Request support

4. Sustain & reflect

Confident, paying it forward

Usability testing, and what it actually changed

5 youth athletes and 3 coordinators, unmoderated remote sessions plus follow-up interviews. These three fixes are the direct result — and they're live in the screens below, not just described here.

Friction

Privacy hesitation

Athletes paused at submit, afraid their coach would see the request.

Fix

A "100% Confidential" badge now sits directly above the submit button.

Friction

No guidance after a low score

Athletes wanted support when they logged low mental readiness.

Fix

Scores below 5 auto-trigger a "Recommended Mental Reset" prompt.

Friction

Unclear pickup process

Athletes didn't know what documentation was needed at pickup.

Fix

A QR verification pass is now built directly into the status tracker.

The coordinator portal

Kendra's whole job in one view — pending requests, available donations, and wellness signals she'd otherwise be tracking across three different spreadsheets.

Accessibility, built in not bolted on

All text and status colors meet WCAG AA contrast against the dark background.

Every tappable element meets a minimum 48×48px touch target.

Interactive components carry screen reader labels throughout.

Copy avoids clinical or alarming language — stigma-free microcopy was a constraint, not an afterthought.

Takeaways & what's next

What I learned

Athletes needed speed and privacy on mobile; coordinators needed density and control on desktop — same underlying data, two genuinely different interfaces. And small decisions, like a confidentiality badge, did more to reduce stigma than any amount of educational copy could.

Next steps

Field validation with local youth community centers, Apple/Google Wallet integration for offline pickup passes, and multi-language support for non-English-speaking families.
