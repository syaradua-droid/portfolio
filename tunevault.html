TuneVault

A vinyl pre-order and collector app that lets fans support several artists in one checkout — choose shipping or local record-shop pickup, and actually see what happened to the order afterward.

Role
UX Designer & Researcher
Platform
Mobile (iOS-style) + Desktop
Tools
Figma, Google Slides
Focus
Research → Hi-Fi Prototype

The problem

"Fans who want to support multiple artists at once are forced to repeat a full checkout for every single album, with no way to track orders once placed."

What research actually said

I interviewed four TuneVault users — a cybersecurity analyst, a college student, a project management intern, and a tattoo artist with a following — to see how they discover and pre-order music today.

Going in, I assumed speed alone would drive pre-order decisions. It didn't. Convenience mattered, but so did feeling recognized — people wanted perks and ways to show support publicly. And once an order was placed, nobody had a reliable way to check on it, which left real excitement about a purchase turning into quiet disconnection.

Fragmented checkout

Pre-ordering meant re-entering payment details and repeating the flow for every artist.

No order tracking

No way to see real-time status once a pre-order was placed.

No loyalty recognition

Frequent supporters got no acknowledgment for pre-ordering often.

No sharing tools

Fans with a following had no built-in way to promote a pre-order.

Meet Monty

27, an audio engineer and self-described collector in Toronto — the persona every decision below traces back to.

"I shouldn't have to place three separate orders just to support an artist's multi-format album drop."

  • Needs — to pre-order from several artists in one sitting, not one checkout per artist.
  • Wants — an at-a-glance view of what's shipped, digital, or still pending.
  • Frustration — bulk, loyal pre-ordering feels transactional, not rewarding.

Monty's journey, mapped

Five stages, from discovering a release to checking his collection weeks later — this is where the cart, the reward moment, and the Collection all came from.

What testing changed

Two rounds of feedback on the cart and reward flow specifically, since those were the two features added directly in response to research.

Round 1

Cart wasn't discoverable

Users didn't notice they could queue more than one album before checkout.

Round 2 fix

A persistent cart icon in the header made the multi-item flow obvious.

Round 1

No way to check an order

After confirming, there was no clear way to see what happened next.

Round 2 fix

A new Collection tab closed the post-order tracking gap entirely.

Round 1

Reward felt disconnected

Some expected recognition right after adding to cart, not after confirming.

Round 2 fix

Kept the Reward screen post-confirmation, but reactions were still positive overall.

Takeaways & what's next

What I learned

A single-item "one-click" checkout looks efficient on paper, but real fans wanted to support several artists in one sitting. Small early assumptions can quietly shape an entire product's information architecture.

Next steps

Validate the new physical-fulfillment flow (Ship to Me vs. Local Pickup) with real users — it hasn't been usability-tested yet — and extend the Recipe A visual system to the homepage and navigation, which currently doesn't match.
